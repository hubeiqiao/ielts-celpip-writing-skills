# Official Sample Case Studies

These cases show how to present official public samples when sharing the writing-coach skills. Do not copy full official sample answers into the package. Link to the source, state the official score, then show how the skill's template should reason from the official examiner comments.

## Case IELTS-A2-7.5: IELTS Academic Task 2 - Official Band 7.5

Source:
- British Council, *Sample Candidate Writing Responses and Examiner Comments*: `https://takeielts.britishcouncil.org/sites/default/files/academic-writing-sample-candidate-responses-and-examiner-comments.pdf`

Sample:
- Official sample: Academic Writing Task 2, Candidate Response 2.
- Official band: 7.5.
- Task summary: the candidate argues about future health and supports a clear position.
- The full sample answer is not reproduced here; use the official PDF.

Skill output demonstration:

1. **Snapshot**
This is a strong response because the position is clear, ideas progress logically, vocabulary is wider, and most sentence control is stable; it remains below the highest bands because development is uneven and some sentence forms are overused.

2. **Task & Word Count**
Academic Task 2. The response meets the 250-word requirement and uses essay format.

3. **Band Estimate & Evidence**

| Criterion | Skill estimate | Evidence from official comment | Why not higher |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Task Response | 7-8 | Clear position, supporting ideas, and an alternative view are present. | One topic area receives too much emphasis, so coverage is not fully balanced. |
| Coherence and Cohesion | 7-8 | Ideas progress logically with effective paragraphing and cohesive devices. | Some long body paragraphs could be broken down. |
| Lexical Resource | 8 | Vocabulary is wide, with less common items and rare spelling/word-formation errors. | Still not a reason to push the whole response to Band 8+. |
| Grammatical Range and Accuracy | 7 | Complex structures and many error-free sentences are present. | Some errors remain, and short sentence forms are overused. |

4. **Criterion Gates**
- Position: pass.
- All parts of task: mostly pass.
- Idea development: pass, with balance issue.
- Paragraph logic: pass.
- Word count: pass.

5. **High-Impact Fixes**
- Balance of ideas -> IELTS Task Response rewards fuller coverage -> add one non-technology reason such as work habits, transportation, or aging populations.
- Paragraph density -> long paragraphs reduce readability -> split body paragraphs by reason.
- Sentence rhythm -> short forms are repeated -> combine selected sentences with contrast or cause-effect clauses.

6. **Reusable Skills**
- Structure: position -> reason -> explanation -> example/effect -> link back.
- Language: use softening language accurately when making broad predictions.
- Habit: before finishing, ask whether one idea has taken too much space.

7. **Calibration Conclusion**
Skill estimate: 7.5. Official band: 7.5. Match.

## Case IELTS-A1-3.5: IELTS Academic Task 1 - Official Band 3.5

Source:
- British Council, *Sample Candidate Writing Responses and Examiner Comments*: `https://takeielts.britishcouncil.org/sites/default/files/academic-writing-sample-candidate-responses-and-examiner-comments.pdf`

Sample:
- Official sample: Academic Writing Task 1, Candidate Response 2.
- Official band: 3.5.
- Task summary: the candidate describes a chart about how children travelled to and from school.
- The full sample answer is not reproduced here; use the official PDF.

Skill output demonstration:

1. **Snapshot**
The low band is justified because the response is under-length, misses a key category, lacks clear progression, and contains frequent spelling and sentence-form errors.

2. **Task & Word Count**
Academic Task 1. The response is significantly below the 150-word minimum, so Task Achievement is capped.

3. **Band Estimate & Evidence**

| Criterion | Skill estimate | Evidence from official comment | Why not higher |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Task Achievement | 3-4 | The task is attempted, but a key feature is missing and the response is under-length. | Missing feature plus word-count failure prevents mid-band task achievement. |
| Coherence and Cohesion | 3 | Information appears, but organisation and progression are hard to identify. | Linking devices are present but inaccurate. |
| Lexical Resource | 3 | Vocabulary is basic, repetitive, and affected by spelling errors. | Word choice does not show enough range or control. |
| Grammatical Range and Accuracy | 3-4 | Some structures are accurate, but errors are frequent and forms are limited. | Frequent sentence-level errors cap the estimate. |

4. **Criterion Gates**
- Overview: weak/unclear.
- Key features: fail, one category omitted.
- Data accuracy: weak.
- Word count: fail.
- No speculation: mostly pass.

5. **High-Impact Fixes**
- Word count -> under-length removes evidence and lowers task credit -> write at least 150 words.
- Key feature selection -> missing chart category lowers TA -> list all categories before writing.
- Sentence control -> frequent errors distract the reader -> use simple accurate comparison frames first.

6. **Reusable Skills**
- Structure: overview -> biggest change -> second comparison -> remaining key feature.
- Language: `increased from...to...`, `fell from...to...`, `remained relatively stable`.
- Habit: check every chart category before submitting.

7. **Calibration Conclusion**
Skill estimate: 3.5. Official band: 3.5. Match.

## Case IELTS-GT1-5.0: IELTS General Training Task 1 - Official Band 5

Source:
- British Council, *General Training Writing Sample Candidate Responses and Examiner Comments*: `https://takeielts.britishcouncil.org/sites/default/files/general-training-writing-sample-candidate-responses-and-examiner-comments.pdf`

Sample:
- Official sample: General Training Writing Task 1, Sample Script A.
- Official band: 5.
- Task summary: the candidate writes a formal letter requesting a room change.
- The full sample answer is not reproduced here; use the official PDF.

Skill output demonstration:

1. **Snapshot**
Band 5 is reasonable because the purpose is clear and the letter format is recognizable, but the answer is short, repeats the task language, and loses control when complex sentences are attempted.

2. **Task & Word Count**
General Training Task 1. The response is below the 150-word minimum, so Task Achievement is capped.

3. **Band Estimate & Evidence**

| Criterion | Skill estimate | Evidence from official comment | Why not higher |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Task Achievement | 5 | Purpose is clear, but content is thin because the response is under-length. | Under-length and repeated prompt language limit task development. |
| Coherence and Cohesion | 5 | Points are present but not always linked well. | Organisation is simple and cohesion is weak. |
| Lexical Resource | 5 | Some appropriate complaint/request language appears. | Repetition and limited range prevent higher LR. |
| Grammatical Range and Accuracy | 5 | Simple sentences work better than attempted complex forms. | Complex structures break down and punctuation is faulty. |

4. **Criterion Gates**
- Letter purpose: pass.
- Bullet coverage: partial pass.
- Tone: formal enough.
- Word count: fail.
- Closing: present but inaccurate.

5. **High-Impact Fixes**
- Length -> IELTS needs enough evidence for all criteria -> add one detail for each bullet.
- Linking -> complaint letters need clear sequence -> use `first`, `as a result`, `therefore`.
- Request frame -> accuracy matters in formal letters -> use `I would be grateful if you could...` correctly.

6. **Reusable Skills**
- Structure: purpose -> problem -> effect -> request.
- Language: `I am writing to express my concern about...`; `I would be grateful if you could...`.
- Habit: count words and expand each bullet before polishing grammar.

7. **Calibration Conclusion**
Skill estimate: 5.0. Official band: 5. Match.

## Case CELPIP-W10: CELPIP Official Writing Level Example

Source:
- CELPIP Writing Scores: `https://www.celpip.ca/prepare-for-celpip/free-resources/writing-scores/`
- CELPIP Test Results and scoring process: `https://www.celpip.ca/take-celpip/test-results/`

Sample:
- Official CELPIP writing pages provide level descriptors, sample responses, and response analysis.
- Use the official page directly for the sample text and level label.
- Do not reproduce the full sample response in this public package.

Skill output demonstration:

1. **Snapshot**
The sample's official level should be used as the anchor, while the skill explains the evidence through Content/Coherence, Vocabulary, Readability, and Task Fulfilment.

2. **Task & Word Count**
Identify whether the sample is Task 1 email or Task 2 survey, then check format and the 150-200 word target.

3. **Scores & Evidence**

| Dimension | Coaching estimate | Evidence to look for in official analysis | Why not higher |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Content/Coherence | Match official level range | Clear purpose, complete task coverage, supported ideas. | Missing detail or weak development would cap this. |
| Vocabulary | Match official level range | Precise, varied, register-appropriate vocabulary. | Repetition or awkward collocation would cap this. |
| Readability | Match official level range | Controlled grammar, transitions, sentence variety, mechanics. | Frequent sentence errors would cap this. |
| Task Fulfilment | Match official level range | Correct format, tone, audience, and task conventions. | Wrong email/survey format would cap this. |

4. **Criterion Gates**
- Task 1: greeting, purpose, body details, request/action, closing.
- Task 2: no greeting/sign-off, choice named, concession, two reasons, balanced ending.

5. **High-Impact Fixes**
- Use the official response analysis to identify the main limiter.
- Convert that limiter into one reusable rule.
- Add one short drill that practises the rule.

6. **Reusable Skills**
- Structure: keep the task-specific format automatic.
- Language: save the official page's strong collocations by function, not by exact topic.
- Habit: compare your draft to the official level analysis, not only the sample wording.

7. **Calibration Conclusion**
The CELPIP official level is the anchor. The public skill should explain why the level is plausible through the four writing dimensions, but it must not claim to reproduce the official rating process.
